# Level 2 — The Ruins Board

## Narrative hook
The archives lead her to a planet covered in the ruins of an extinct civilization. An alien nomad — the last keeper of that memory — says he's seen objects appear and disappear in anomalies like Stella's. He knows someone who might know more. But first, he demands she play the sacred game of the ancient builders.

## Level context
Stella lands on a planet covered in the ruins of an extinct civilization. There, a nomadic alien challenges her to the sacred game of the ancient builders. It's played on a ceremonial stone board, whose one unbreakable rule is to respect the immovable stone at its center.

## Overview
A Combinatorial Game Theory duel on a grid. The player faces a problem of symmetric positions where visual, geometric strategy is the key to winning.

## Rules
- The board is a 7x7 grid with the center square blocked (48 free squares total).
- The alien and Stella take turns placing an "L"-shaped stone piece (which covers 4 squares, similar to a chess knight's silhouette).
- Pieces can be rotated and reflected, but can't overlap or go outside the board's limits.
- The alien always places the first piece. The player who can no longer place a piece loses.

## Strategy
Victory depends on the "Mirror Strategy". Since the center square is blocked, the board has perfect symmetry. As the second player, Stella only needs to respond to each of the alien's moves by placing her piece in the exact mirror position, rotated 180° about the center. If the opponent finds a valid spot, the opposite symmetric position will always be free, guaranteeing Stella never runs out of moves.

## Why it works
The strategy is visual and remarkably elegant, moving away from numeric calculations. The "aha moment" happens when the player understands the level's design: the central stone isn't there by accident; it stops the opponent from crossing the center and breaking the mirror. Once the symmetry is grasped, the strategy is infallible and removes any element of luck.
